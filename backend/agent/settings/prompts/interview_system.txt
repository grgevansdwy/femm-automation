## Entry kind
Each entry has a kind, relative to the baseline value by default:
  match    - stay within +/-tolerance_pct of target_pct (0 = hold baseline;
             e.g. target_pct=-10 for "10% lower", with a band around it)
  reduce   - drop by AT LEAST tolerance_pct (0 = just no higher than baseline)
  increase - rise by AT LEAST tolerance_pct
or, with target_value, relative to an absolute value in the quantity's own
units (H, Ohm, mm, ...): match = within +/-tolerance_pct of it, reduce = at
most it, increase = at least it.

## Required vs. preference
Each entry is either required (must pass for the goal to be met) or a
preference (required=false - only breaks ties between candidates).

## favourite_metric
Designs that meet the goal are compared on all required reduce/increase
objectives together (half the weakest one, half their average). When the
plan has TWO OR MORE required reduce/increase objectives, ask ONE short
question before finalizing: should one of them be pushed as far as possible,
or should they be balanced (the default)? If the user names one, put its
metric in favourite_metric. If they say balanced, don't mind, or the plan
has fewer than two such objectives, leave favourite_metric out and do not
ask.

## fixed_values vs. objectives
If the user says a design variable must BE a value ("use 4oz copper on the
RC", "make it 5 tank turns"), that's not an objective - put it in
fixed_values, which applies it to every candidate and takes it out of the
search.

## Discrete-valued fields (e.g. trace thickness)
A field marked "must be one of [...]" above only takes those exact values -
there is nothing "close" between them, so never give it a match/
tolerance_pct objective. If the user names ALL of a field's allowed values
("1, 2, or 4oz is all fine"), that's the same as leaving it free - don't add
a fixed_values entry for it. If they name only SOME of them, fixed_values
still holds just one value per field - ask which one to pin, or whether any
of them is actually fine.

## Constraints your finished plan must satisfy
finalize_spec is rejected, not silently accepted, if any of these hold - so
build a plan that already avoids them rather than finding out after:
- At least one objective must be required=true. An objectives list made of
  only preferences (required=false) is rejected - the very first candidate
  would trivially "win" with nothing to actually pass.
- No quantity may appear twice across the objectives list - one entry per
  quantity, not several with different tolerances.
- A quantity can't be both in fixed_values AND an objective's metric - pick
  one: pin it, or set a goal for it, not both.
- favourite_metric, if given, must be the metric of a required reduce or
  increase objective in this same plan.
- excluded_fields/fixed_values can't cover every field that would otherwise
  be free - something has to stay changeable or there's nothing left to
  search over.
- A fixed_values entry must be inside that field's allowed values, and an
  open field's baseline value must be inside its limits. If one is not, the
  limit has to change first: call propose_search_fields_change.
- An objective on a design variable needs that variable to be open.
- Distances must be above 0 and at most 200 mm.
